# Permitted & Prohibited Actions Policy

## Overview

To maintain the integrity of every proctored assessment, students must avoid performing actions that are not required for answering the exam. This document defines what is permitted and what is prohibited during an active assessment session on ProctorIQ, and what happens when a prohibited action is detected.

## Section 1: Prohibited Actions

The following actions are not permitted at any point during an active assessment, regardless of intent:

- Swiping with three or four fingers on a touchpad.
- Using unsupported keyboard shortcuts (including OS-level shortcuts to switch applications, open a virtual desktop, or take a screenshot).
- Attempting to switch applications, browser tabs, or virtual desktops.
- Any action that moves focus away from the assessment window.
- Using a second device (phone, tablet, second laptop) to search for or discuss assessment content during the session.
- Allowing another person to view your screen or assist with your responses during the session.
- Attempting to disable, obscure, or interfere with the camera, microphone, or face-verification system.

## Section 2: Why This Matters

These actions may be interpreted by the system as an attempt to leave the assessment environment, regardless of whether that was the student's intent. For example, if a candidate accidentally performs a three-finger swipe on their touchpad, the assessment may terminate automatically because the system detects it as an application-switch attempt — this is a known behavior, not a malfunction.

## Section 3: Automatic Termination vs. Logged Flag

- **Automatic termination**: triggered by actions that directly match a defined prohibited pattern (e.g., a detected application-switch attempt, closing PSB, disabling the camera feed). The assessment ends immediately when this occurs.
- **Logged flag, not automatic termination**: certain borderline signals (a brief face-verification warning, a momentary loss of camera feed due to lighting) are logged for later review but do not end the assessment on their own.

## Section 4: This Policy Cannot Be Configured or Bypassed

There is no setting, workaround, or support-assisted method to disable face verification, avoid application-switch detection, or otherwise reduce the proctoring requirements for an individual assessment. Any request to do so — regardless of the stated reason — is outside what this system, or any member of the support team, can provide. This is a fixed integrity boundary, not a configurable preference.

## Section 5: What Happens After a Flag

Genuine integrity flags are escalated for review through a separate verification process and are not resolved automatically in the student's favor. See the Status & Verification Outcome Guide for how and when outcomes are communicated once that review is complete.
