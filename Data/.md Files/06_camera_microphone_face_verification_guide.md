# During-Assessment Guide: Camera, Microphone & Face Verification

## Overview

ProctorIQ uses continuous camera-based face verification and, for some assessment formats, microphone monitoring throughout your session. This document covers what to do if you receive a warning related to any of these during an active assessment.

## Section 1: Responding to a Warning

If you receive a warning related to your camera, microphone, or face verification while assessing:
- Ensure your face is clearly and fully visible to the camera.
- Confirm you are in a well-lit environment — avoid sitting with a light source directly behind you.
- Verify that camera and microphone permissions are still enabled for PSB (permissions can occasionally be revoked by an OS update mid-session on some devices).
- Follow any on-screen instructions displayed alongside the warning, which may differ slightly by warning type.

## Section 2: Common Causes of Repeated Warnings

- **Face partially out of frame**: reposition your webcam or your seating so your full face remains visible for the duration of the assessment.
- **Backlighting**: a bright window or light source behind you causes the system to lose a clear read on your face. Face a light source rather than sitting with your back to it.
- **Multiple faces detected**: only the assessing student should be visible in the camera frame at any point during the assessment.
- **Camera obstruction**: ensure nothing (a sticky note, laptop camera cover, hand) is blocking the lens.

## Section 3: The Difference Between a Warning and a Termination

A single or occasional warning is logged but does not end your assessment. Repeated or unresolved warnings, however, may be escalated and reviewed as part of the assessment's integrity check — see the Permitted & Prohibited Actions Policy document for what specifically triggers automatic termination versus a logged warning.

## Section 4: What This Document Does Not Cover

This document does not determine whether a specific flagged warning will affect your assessment outcome — that determination is made through a separate verification review process, not disclosed to the student in real time during the assessment. See the Status & Verification Outcome Guide for how outcomes are eventually communicated.
